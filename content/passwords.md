## The basics

Use a different password for every account. If one account is breached, your other accounts stay safe.

Long is better than complex. A passphrase made of three or four random words beats a short password with symbols swapped in. For example, *purple bicycle elephant window* is stronger and easier to remember than *P@ssw0rd!123*.

Never share your password. No SCU department will ever ask you for it. If someone does, it is a scam.

## Create a strong passphrase

<div class="steps">

1. Pick three or four unrelated words. Avoid song lyrics, names, and dates.
2. Join them with spaces or a separator you will remember.
3. Make it at least 14 characters long.
4. Use a password manager so you do not have to remember it.

</div>

## Use a password manager

A password manager stores all your passwords and fills them in for you. You only remember one master password.

- **Bitwarden** — free, works on phone and desktop.
- **Built-in** — most phones and browsers (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, Chrome) have one already.

Your SCU password should be unique — do not reuse it for personal accounts.

## Changing your SCU password

If you need to change your SCU password, go to <a href="https://mysignins.microsoft.com/security-info">mysignins.microsoft.com/security-info</a> and select **Password**.

If you think your password has been stolen, change it immediately and contact the Technology Services Service Desk.

## What to avoid

- Reusing passwords across accounts
- Using personal details (name, birthday, address)
- Short passwords under 12 characters
- Writing passwords on sticky notes
- Saving passwords in unencrypted files
